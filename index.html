01 — The Problem

Why Do We Purify Water?

Water from rivers, lakes, and wells often contains harmful substances. Purification removes or destroys these contaminants so the water is safe to drink.

Pathogens

Bacteria (E. coli, Cholera), viruses, and parasites that cause waterborne diseases.

Chemical Pollutants

Heavy metals (lead, mercury), pesticides, fertilisers, and industrial waste dissolved in water.

Sediment & Turbidity

Suspended particles of clay, silt, and organic matter that make water cloudy — measured as turbidity.

Dissolved Salts

Minerals like calcium and magnesium that cause hardness, and salt in seawater that makes it undrinkable.

Distillation Process

Distillation separates water from dissolved substances by boiling and condensing. Press the button to run the animation step by step.

1

Heating begins. The water in the flask warms toward its boiling point.

2

At 100°C, water turns to steam, leaving dissolved salts and impurities behind.

3

Steam travels through the delivery tube into the condenser.

4

Cold water in the condenser jacket cools the steam, condensing it back to liquid.

5

Pure distilled water drips into the collection flask — free of all dissolved substances.

05 — The Science

The Chemistry Behind Purification

Several purification methods rely on chemical reactions. Here are the key equations, beautifully typeset with KaTeX.

Disinfection

Chlorination

Chlorine gas reacts with water to form hypochlorous acid, a powerful disinfectant that kills bacteria by destroying cell membranes.

HOCl (hypochlorous acid) penetrates bacterial cell walls and oxidises enzymes, killing the organism.

Coagulation

Alum Treatment

Aluminium sulfate (alum) is added to muddy water. It reacts to form a sticky precipitate that traps fine particles.

Al(OH)₃ forms a floc — a fluffy mass that settles to the bottom, carrying dirt with it.

Softening

Ion Exchange

Hard water contains Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions. An ion-exchange resin swaps these for harmless Na⁺ ions, softening the water.

The resin (R) is regenerated by flushing with concentrated salt solution.

Neutralisation

pH Adjustment

Acidic water is treated with lime (calcium hydroxide) to raise its pH to a safe level for consumption.

The ideal pH for drinking water is between 6.5 and 8.5.

08 — Key Points

Key Takeaways

1

No single method is enough. Real water treatment combines coagulation, filtration, disinfection, and pH adjustment.

2

Filtration removes particles, not dissolved chemicals. Activated charcoal adsorbs many organic compounds.

3

Distillation produces the purest water but is slow and energy-intensive — impractical for large-scale supply.

4

Chlorination is the most common disinfection method worldwide because it's cheap, effective, and provides residual protection.

5

Chemistry is central — from precipitation reactions in coagulation to redox in chlorination and ion exchange in softening.
